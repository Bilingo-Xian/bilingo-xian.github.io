GE8 U2C7

Different Tastes

Unit 2 · Food and Drink

💡 Main Idea — what students should be able to do

Demonstrate a taste-check exchange: each student independently says “[This lemonade] is too [sour].” / “[These waffles] are not [sweet] enough.” about a pictured food.

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] Too Much Sugar!: the teacher mimes tasting last lesson’s kebab — “I think it tastes…”; then mimes spooning ten spoons of sugar into lemonade and drinking it — a disgusted face: “It is…? Hmm, what’s the word?”; the co-teacher answers wrong (“Very sweet! Perfect!”); students correct in any language; land on “This lemonade is too sweet.” — then a tiny sip face for “not sweet enough”.
  • [Topical Discussion] Sweet, Sour, Salty: ask students which foods they know that are sweet, sour or salty; accept gestures (yummy/yucky faces) and one-word answers in any language, then connect them to the taste words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. How are Pili's fries? -> They are too salty.
  • 2. What's wrong with Lele's waffles? -> They aren't sweet enough.
B
  • 1.Why doesn't Laki like the taste of coffee? -> It's too bitter.
  • 2. What food does Ola like? -> He likes sweet and salty food.
C
  • 1. Is Ola a good friend? Is he polite?-> No, he eats all his friends' foods. / Accept students' own answers
  • 2. Is it healthy to eat so much sweet food? Why or why not? -> Accept students' own answers.

📐 Grammar Focus

1. too + adjective/adverb (excessively); too many/too much + noun. Also: too at the end of an affirmative sentence means "also" (e.g. "I like it, too."). 2. not + adjective/adverb + enough; enough + noun.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • This coffee is too sweet.
B
  • These waffles are not sweet enough.

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Blind Taste Test — put the review food cards (beef, lamb, pork, grapes, strawberry, kebab) face down in pile A and the flavour cards (sugar, syrup, salt, lemon, coffee) face down in pile B (food-and-flavour word cards are in the GE8 U2C7 materials folder); a student draws one card from each pile (e.g. lamb + syrup): “Congratulations, you are eating lamb with syrup. How does it taste?” — “I think it tastes terrible! It is too sweet.”
  2. Too Much! Mimes — the teacher mimes actions with clearly wrong amounts (a whole salt shaker on food, one tiny spoon of sugar in tea, the whole bottle of syrup on waffles). The students produce target structures based on the miming.
  3. Flavor Corners — name the room corners SWEET, SOUR, SALTY and BITTER; call out foods (“Lemonade!” “Coffee!” “Grapes!”); students move to a corner and say the sentence (“Lemonade is too sour for me.” / “Coffee is too bitter.”); ask one student per corner why.
  4. Too Sour (HTML Interactive) -- students choose the correct description of the food on TV, then make a sentence. They can also tell if they like or don't like it. (see below)

🏠 Homework Ideas

  1. Using the teacher's example, record a dialogue with your parents or friends using a target structure.
  2. Read the words, the dialogue and the story from the book. Extension 1: make your own sentences with the target vocabulary and record them for the teacher. Extension 2: read the story with expression and/or role play the story with your friends or parents.
  3. Extension: try a food or drink you've never had before. Express your opinion about it ("These are waffles. I think they taste terrible… They are too sweet! I don't like sweet food, because it is bad for you.").
