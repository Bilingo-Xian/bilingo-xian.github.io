Say Hello

Unit 1 · Ready For School

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Topical Discussion]* Teacher and Co-teacher Introductions: greet the co-teacher and ask "What's your name?"; invite students to greet both teachers and share their own names — accept a single-word name or a wave.
  • [Interactive Game] Hello or 你好?: the teacher and co-teacher give greetings in English and Chinese; ask "English or Chinese?" Then invite students to say hello in English.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. What is his name? [Point to Ola.] -> Ola.
  • 2. Does Ola speak Chinese or English at first? [Point at the sentence] -> Chinese.
B
  • 1. Why does the teacher say "Good job, Ola!"? -> Ola answers in English / says "My name is Ola."
C
  • 1. How do you think Ola feels at the end? Why? -> Possible answer: Happy, because the teacher says "Good job." Accept other picture-supported interpretations.
  • 2. What could you say to welcome a new classmate like Ola? -> Possible answers: Hello!; My name is [own name].; What's your name?

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
nameEnglish
B
Chineseaskspeak
C
question

📐 Grammar Focus

No grammar focus for this lesson.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • My name is [name].
B
  • What's your name?

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. After vocabulary teaching, with a short repeat during speaking practice. Prepare printed cards or flashcards for target vocabulary. Students pick a random card. They read the selected word aloud. They use it in their own short sentence, e.g. "My name is (Eric)".
  2. Reading the story expansion (HTML Interactive): match story sentences to panels; sort examples by language — Chinese or English. (see below)
  3. Story Role-Play: after the second reading, act as the teacher and Ola, then substitute students' own names.

In My Bag

Unit 1 · Ready For School

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] The Co-teacher's Empty Bag: the co-teacher panics — "Oh no, school starts! What's in my bag? Nothing!"; the teacher finds classroom objects one by one and asks "For school?" and "What's this?"; students help choose what to pack by pointing or naming — the teacher gives the English word each time.
  • [Interactive Game] Teacher's Mystery Bag: show a closed bag and ask "What's in my bag?"; let students guess, then reveal the objects and ask "What's this?"; accept one-word replies in any language ("pen!", "铅笔!") before modelling the full answer.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. What's this? [Point to the bag, then the pencil case.] -> It's a bag. / It's a pencil case.
  • 2. What's in the pencil case? -> A ruler, an eraser, a pen and a marker.
B
  • 1. Why do they open the pencil case? -> They want to see what is in it.
  • 2. Is the pencil case empty? / Does the pencil case have anything? How do you know? -> It's not empty. There's a ruler / an eraser / a pen / a marker inside.
C
  • 1. What do you put in your pencil case? -> Possible answers: A pen, an eraser, a ruler. Accept students' own choices.
  • 2. [Draw a wiggly line on the whiteboard.] Oh no! What do I need? -> An eraser. A ruler.
  • 3. Your friend has no pen. What can you do? -> Possible answers: Give my pen to them.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
bageraserpenruler
B
pencil casemarker

📐 Grammar Focus

1. In speech, answer What's this? with It's a/an…; this refers to a person or thing nearby. 2. Use a before words beginning with a consonant sound and an before words beginning with a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u), e.g. a pen, an eraser, an apple. 3. Match pronouns with be: He/She/It is…

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • What's this?
  • It's a/an [object].
B
  • It's a/an [object].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. A/An Sorting Game (HTML interactive): students sort target and familiar review vocabulary into a/an sentences, then say the sentences. (see below)
  2. What's in the Bag?: the teacher hides a classroom object in a bag and asks "What's this?"; a student feels the object without looking and guesses using "It's a/an [...]".
  3. Musical Pass: students pass a pencil case containing classroom objects; when the music stops, the student holding it takes out one object and says "It's a/an [...]".

My Classroom

Unit 1 · Ready For School

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] Near Teacher, Far Co-teacher: hold a familiar object nearby while the co-teacher holds another across the room; ask "What's this?" and "What's that?"; swap positions and repeat — the teachers do the talking, students listen and point, and the near/far contrast creates the need for both questions.
  • [Topical Discussion] Classroom Look Around: ask "What can you see?"; point to familiar classroom objects and ask "What's this?"; students name what they already know — accept one-word answers in English or Chinese — before learning the new words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. What's this? [Point to the computer while standing next to the TV.] -> It's a computer.
  • 2. What's that? [Take a step away from the TV and point to the computer again.] -> That's a computer.
B
  • 1. What do Laki and Lele do? -> They draw their classroom.
  • 2. What is on the white board? -> Letters "A", "B", "C".
C
  • 1. Do the kids look happy or sad? Why? -> They are happy because they can draw together.
  • 2. What's in our classroom? What about your classroom at school? -> Answer according to the situation.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
chairdoorcomputerwindowdesk
B
whiteboard

📐 Grammar Focus

1. In speech, answer What's that? with It's a/an…; that refers to a person or thing farther away. 2. Review a/an. 3. Distinguish this/that in exercises.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • What's that?
  • That's a/an [object].
  • Review and contrast: What's this?
  • It's a/an [object].
B
  • That's a/an [object].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Near or Far?: place objects or flashcards near students and farther away; partners point, ask "What's this/that?" and answer "It's a/an [...]".
  2. Classroom Detectives: students take turns pointing to classroom objects farther away and asking a partner "What's that?".
  3. Move and Ask: show an object nearby, then move it farther away; students change their question from "What's this?" to "What's that?" and answer.

Good and Bad

Unit 1 · Ready For School

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation]* Co-teacher Mini-scenes: the teacher and co-teacher mime actions (listening, chatting during the lesson); ask "Good or bad?" and "Is it fun?"; accept short answers and gestures before modelling the full target sentences.
  • [Topical Discussion] What Is Fun?: ask "What do you like to do?"; students choose or mime an activity — accept gestures and single words — and ask "Is it fun?" to connect their interests to the lesson.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. How many kids can you see? -> I can see four kids.
  • 2. Is teacher happy or unhappy with Ola? -> She is unhappy with Ola.
B
  • 1. Is Ola a good student? Why? -> No, he isn't. / No, Ola is a bad student. He chats and yells in class.
  • 2. Why is teacher unhappy with Ola? -> He chats and yells in class.
C
  • 1. Are you a good student or a bad student? Why?
  • 2. What else is good, bad or fun to do in class?

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
badgoodfun
B
yellchatcalm down

📐 Grammar Focus

1. It's good to + verb. 2. It's bad to + verb. 3. It's fun to + verb.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • It's good to [verb/verb phrase].
  • It's bad to [verb/verb phrase].
  • It's fun to [verb/verb phrase].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Good, Bad or Fun?: show pictures of actions in different situations (see the cloud folder); students choose a label and say "It's good/bad/fun to [...]". Accept more than one suitable label. (see below)
  2. Good Side / Bad Side: split the room in half: one side is GOOD, one side is BAD. Teacher calls out an action: "Yell!"-> kids run to the BAD side; "Listen!"-> kids run to the GOOD side. First, they repeat sentences together; in the second half of the game, the last kid to the correct side has to say the sentence solo.
  3. Build a Sentence: teacher prepares word cards; students arrange them into "It's good/bad/fun to [...]" sentences, read them aloud, then change the action or situation.

At Pili's House

Unit 2 · My Family and Friends

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] Meet My Family: the teacher shows (or quickly draws) a simple family picture and introduces it — "This is my mother. This is my father."; the co-teacher plays a visitor who keeps asking "Who's this?"; students answer with the family word ("mother", "father" or Chinese accepted) or repeat the full sentence if they can.
  • [Topical Discussion] Who Brought You?: ask students who brought them to school or who picks them up; accept one-word replies in any language, then connect them to the family words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. [Point at Pili's mother.] You are Pili. Who's this? -> This is my mother.
  • 2. Who is on the sofa? -> Pili's grandmother. Pili's grandfather.
  • 3. Where are they? -> At Pili's house.
B
  • 1. Who says "This is my mother."? -> Pili.
  • 2. How many family members does Pili have? -> Four.
  • 3. Is the grandmother young or old? -> Old.
C
  • 1. How many people are in your family? Who are they? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. What can you say when you meet a friend's family? -> Possible answers: Hello!; Nice to meet you.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
fathermothergrandfathergrandmotherhousenice
B
meetNote: also accept grandma/grandpa for grandmother/grandfather.
C
Nice to meet you

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Understand who in speech. 2. Who's = Who is. 3. Answer Who is this? with This is my… (know the meaning of my).

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • Who's this?
  • This is my [family member].
B
  • This is his/her/[name]'s [family member].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Mystery Family Game (Flashcard Flip) — place family member flashcards face-down on the board. Students take turns choosing a card; before flipping it, the class asks "Who's this?" The student flips it and answers "This is my [father]." Correct answers keep the card; most cards wins.
  2. Who's this? Game (HTML) — eight face-down family cards on screen. Students choose a card, the class asks "Who's this?", and the student flips it and answers "This is my [...]." Use "Add Difficulty!" to mix in classroom objects and "Who's that?"/"What's this/that?" questions. (see below)
  3. Family Photo Tour — students bring or draw a simple family picture, walk around the room and introduce two family members to each partner using "This is my [...]."
  4. Meet the Family Role-Play — the co-teacher plays a visitor; students take turns introducing their "family" (flashcards on the board) with "This is my [...]"; encourage "Nice to meet you."

My Family Photo

Unit 2 · My Family and Friends

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] One or Two?: the teacher shows a family photo and reviews the last lesson — "This is my father." "This is my mother." — then points at both parents together and stalls on purpose: "This is my…? Hmm, no — they! Who are they?"; take hints in any language from the students and complete the sentence yourself: "They are my parents."
  • [Topical Discussion] Ask students about their parents and grandparents — who they live with or see at weekends; accept one-word answers in any language, then connect them to the target words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. Does Nani love her family? -> Yes, she does.
  • 2. [Point at the photo.] What does Nani show to Laki? -> She shows her (family) photo.
  • 3. How is Nani's mother? -> She's beautiful and young.
  • 4. How many people are in Nani's family? -> Five: Nani, her parents and her grandparents.
B
  • 1. Who says "Yes, she is young."? -> Nani does.
  • 2. Who is beautiful and young? -> The girl's mother.
C
  • 1. Do you think your mother/teacher is young or old? -> Accept students' own opinions.
  • 2. What do you love about your family? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 3. Who looks young in your family? Who looks old? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
beautifullovephotoyoungold
B
grandparentsparents

📐 Grammar Focus

1. parents = mother + father; grandparents = grandfather + grandmother; singular means one, plural means two or more. 2. Answer Who are they? with They are my… 3. Agreement with be: They are…

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • Who are they?
  • They are my [family members].
  • Review: Who's this? — This is my [family member].
B
  • They are his/her/[name]'s [family members].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Who's this vs What's this — Spin the Wheel (HTML) — spin the wheel to show family members or classroom objects; students ask and answer "Who's this/that?" — "This/That is my [...]" or "What's this/that?" — "It's/That's a [...]". Play "Family only" first, then "Add Classroom Objects" for review. (see below)
  2. Photo Guess — students look at a partner's simple family drawing for ten seconds, then ask "Who are they?"; the partner answers "They are my [...]."
  3. Family Tree Sort — groups arrange family word cards (father, mother, parents, grandfather, grandmother, grandparents) into a tree on the board and present each pair with "They are my [...]."

Let's Look in the Mirror

Unit 2 · My Family and Friends

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] And Me?: the teacher reviews the family photo — "Who are they? They are my parents. They are old" — then looks at themselves and stalls: "And me? I… am?"; take hints in any language from the students and complete it yourself: "I'm young." — using gestures to help.
  • [Topical Discussion] Who Is Tall?: the teacher and co-teacher stand back to back and ask the class "Who is tall? Who is short?"; students point and answer with a name or "you!"; accept pointing and single words, then connect to the adjectives.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. You are Nani. Describe yourself. / What do you look like? -> I am little.
  • 2. Who is big and fat? -> Pili is.
  • 3. How many kids are there? -> There're three kids.
B
  • 1. Is it fun to look in the mirror? -> It is fun to look in the mirror.
  • 2. Is Nani big or little? -> Little.
  • 3. How is Pili? -> Pili is big and fat.
C
  • 1. Are the children happy? How do you know? -> Possible answers: Yes. They are laughing/smiling.
  • 2. How would you describe yourself? -> Accept students' own answers, e.g. "I'm tall."
  • 3. Who in the story is like you? Why? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
biglittleshorttallthinfat
C
mirror

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Words for describing people (e.g. tall, fat). 2. Oral review of This is / They are. 3. Learn I am; I'm = I am. 4. Review be verbs: am / is / are.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • I'm [name].
  • I'm [adjective/adjectives].
  • Review: Who are they? — They are my [family members].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Mirror Self-intro — take the class to the mirror in turns; each student looks in the mirror and records a self-introduction: "I'm [name]. I'm [adjective]."
  2. Body Adjective TPR Game — call out "big!" and students puff up and stretch wide, "little!" they crouch tiny, "tall!" they stand on tiptoes with arms up, "short!" they squat down, "thin!" they squeeze in sideways, "fat!" they puff out their cheeks and spread their arms. Speed up until it gets chaotic and funny, then flip it: one student does a pose and the others shout the word.
  3. Opposites Action Chorus — the teacher calls opposite pairs (big-little, tall-short, thin-fat); the class acts out each word and chants it, then individual students give commands for the class to perform.

Our Friends

Unit 2 · My Family and Friends

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] I'm Tall… No!: the teacher reviews self-descriptions — "I'm tall." — then points at the co-teacher and stalls on purpose: "I'm tall? No! He…? She…?"; take hints in any language from the students and build the sentence yourself: "She is tall and kind." — the new adjectives are the solution.
  • [Interactive Game]* Guess Who: the teacher describes a student in the class with known words and gestures; the class guesses who it is — accept names and pointing; keep the describing to the teacher so students only listen and guess.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. Who is strong and kind? -> Pili is.
  • 2. What does Pili carry? -> A lot of books.
  • 3. How is Ola? / What is Ola like? / Describe Ola. -> Ola is funny and helpful.
B
  • 1. Why is Pili strong? -> He can carry a lot of books.
  • 2. Why is Ola helpful? -> He helps his Mom with the dishes.
  • 3. Can you describe Lele and Nani? -> Lele is short and smart. Nani is funny and kind.
C
  • 1. Is Pili a good friend? Why? -> Possible answers: Yes. He helps others.
  • 2. Who helps you at school? How? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 3. Can you describe your friends at school? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
helpfulkindstrong
B
funnypolitesmart

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Oral review of be patterns: This is / They are / I am. 2. Learn He/She/It/name + is. 3. He's = He is; She's = She is.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • [Name] is [adjective] and [adjective].
B
  • Review and contrast: I'm [name]. I'm [adjective].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Adjective Slap — write each adjective (funny, helpful, kind, polite, smart, strong) on a separate card and spread them face-up on the table. Two students stand on each side, hands behind their backs. The teacher acts out or gives a clue, e.g. flexes muscles; both race to slap the correct card ("strong!"). The first to slap says the full sentence, e.g. "Daddy is strong and helpful!" The winner keeps the card; most cards wins.
  2. Guess My Classmate — one student thinks of a classmate and gives two clues: "She is kind and funny." The class guesses until they can get a correct answer.
  3. Two Words, One Person (HTML) — Student 1 flips a name card and a family card and reveals the question "How is/are [Name]'s [family]?"; Student 2 flips two adjective cards and answers "[Name]'s [family] is/are [adj1] and [adj2]." Draw again for a new pair. (see below)
  4. Compliment Circle — in a circle, each student describes a classmate with two adjectives: "[Name] is [kind] and [smart]." The class confirms with a thumbs up.

Where's My Rabbit?

Unit 3 · At Home

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] Hide-and-Seek with a toy or a doll: ask the kids to close their eyes and hide it somewhere obvious in the classroom; ask "Where is it?"; students point and answer — accept one-word replies ("door!", "desk!") before modelling the full answer.
  • [Topical Discussion] Ask students where they sleep, eat or play at home; accept one-word answers in any language, then connect them to the target room words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. [Point at the character.] Who is it? -> It's Lele.
  • 2. Where is the rabbit? -> She's in the garden.
B
  • 1. What is Lele looking for? / What does Lele want to find? -> Her rabbit.
  • 2. Where does Lele look? -> Lele looks in the bedroom, the bathroom, the living room, and the dining room.
C
  • 1. What pets do you have? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. What rooms are there in your house? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
bathroombedroomgardenkitchenliving roomroom
B
dining room

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Answer Where is…? with …is in the + room. 2. where asks about place; where's = where is. 3. Use in the + room name.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • Where is she?
  • She's in the [room].
  • Where is he?
  • He's in the [room].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Where Is She? Where Is He? (HTML) — a character appears in a room of the house; students ask and answer "Where is she/he?" — "She's/He's in the [room]." Cover all six rooms and both pronouns. (see below)
  2. Find the Rabbit — place the room flashcards around the classroom. Ask the kids to close their eyes and hide a rabbit flashcard behind one of the room flashcards; ask "Where's the rabbit?"; students guess "It's in the [room]." (or "is it in the [room]?" for higher level students). Until it is found, then a student hides it again and leads the game.
  3. Room Corners — put the six room cards in different corners or on different tables; the teacher or co-teacher goes to a corner; students ask "Where is he/she?" and answer in full sentences.
  4. Guess My Room — one student chooses a room card without showing it to anyone; the class asks "Are you in the kitchen?"; the student answers "Yes." or "No."; when the room is guessed, the student says "I'm in the [room]."

The Dollhouse

Unit 3 · At Home

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] Sleep on a Desk?!: the teacher reviews the rooms with mimes — sleep, eat, cook — and students call out the rooms; then the teacher mimes sleeping on a desk and looks puzzled: "Sleep on a desk?! No! Where do I sleep? What's in the bedroom?"; let the problem lead to the new furniture words.
  • [Topical Discussion] Your Room: ask students what things they have in their bedroom at home; accept one-word answers in English or Chinese, then connect them to the furniture words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. [Point at the dollhouse]. What is it? -> It's a dollhouse.
  • 2. What is in the bedroom? -> A bed is in the bedroom.
B
  • 1. What two things are in the living room? -> A table and a sofa are in the living room.
  • 2. Does Nani like her dollhouse? Why? -> Yes, she does. Because it's beautiful/fun/etc.
C
  • 1. What's in your kitchen/living room/bathroom at your house? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. Do you have a dollhouse? What's in your dollhouse? -> Accept students' own anwers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
bedtable
B
fridgesinksofastovedollhouse

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Review a/an within the vocabulary. 2. What's in the…? — A + singular noun is in the…; A + singular noun and a + singular noun are in the…; number + plural noun are in the… (optional, depending on the class). 3. what asks about things; what's = what is.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • What's in the [room]?
  • A/An [item] is in the [room].
  • Review: — Where is she/he? — She's/He's in the [room].
B
  • A [bed] and a [table] are in the [room].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Paper Dollhouse — use a printable dollhouse with room and furniture cards (or a real dollhouse if one is available); groups furnish the rooms and present each room with "A [bed] is in the [bedroom]."
  2. What’s Missing? (HTML) — students study a furnished room, then spot the missing objects and answer “What’s missing?” Reveal the objects and practise “A chair is in the room” or “A table and a chair are in the room.” Add difficulty with shuffled furniture, multiple missing objects, and funny emoji surprises. (see below)
  3. Furniture Sentence Builders — show two furniture cards and a room card; students arrange word cards into "A [...] and a [...] are in the [...]." and read the sentence aloud.
  4. Draw and Describe — each student draws one room with two or three items, then describes it to a partner using the target structure; partners ask "What's in the [room]?"

Around the House

Unit 3 · At Home

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] Wrong Room!: the teacher reviews the rooms with quick mimes — sleep, eat, cook — and students call out the rooms; then the teacher say "Kitchen" and mimes sleeping and looks puzzled: "Sleep in the kitchen?! No! What do I do in the kitchen?"; the mistake creates the need for the new action words, which the teacher supplies.
  • [Topical Discussion] Ask students what they do at home in the morning and in the evening; accept gestures and short answers in any language, then connect them to the target actions.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. [Point at the picture]. Where's Pili? -> Pili is in the kitchen.
  • 2. You are Pili's father. What do you do in the living room? -> I read the newspaper in the living room.
B
  • 1. Does Pili love his mom? How do you know? -> Yes, he does. Because they hug!
  • 2. What does Pili's mom watch on TV? -> She watches animals on TV.
C
  • 1. What do you do in the living room/kitchen/bathroom/etc. -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. What does your mother/father do in [the room]? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
cookhairnewspaperTVwatch
B
dishes

📐 Grammar Focus

1. What do you do in the + room? — I… in the + room. (Teach the verbs as phrases.)

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • What do you do in the [room]?
  • I [action] in the [room].
  • Review: — What's in the [room]? — A/An [item] is in the [room].
B
  • He/She [actions] in the [room].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Around the House (HTML) — a character appears in a room doing an action (the character pictures are in the same folder as the file); students ask and answer "What do you do in the [room]?" — "I [action] in the [room]." using full sentences. (see below)
  2. Mime the Action — one student mimes a house action; the others ask "What do you do in the [room]?" and the student answers with the full sentence.
  3. Action-Room Match Relay — teams match action cards to the correct room cards on the board, then say one full sentence per match.
  4. My Saturday — students tell a partner three true sentences about their own home: "I [action] in the [room]."; partners report one sentence back to the class.

Let's Clean the Bathroom

Unit 3 · At Home

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] Oh No, What Do We Do?: the teacher "accidentally" drops some flashcards or paper scraps on the floor; ask the co-teacher "Oh no! What do we do?"; the co-teacher mimes cleaning; accept short answers and gestures, then introduce the target question.
  • [Topical Discussion] Who Cleans at Home?: ask students who cleans at their home and what they clean; accept gestures, one-word answers and Chinese, then connect them to the cleaning words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. You are Ola. Do you want to clean the bath? -> No, I don't.
  • 2. What does Ola want to clean? -> His hands. / He wants to clean his hands.
B
  • 1. Does Ola want to help his mom? Why or why not? -> No, he doesn't. He thinks the bathroom is too dirty.
  • 1. How is Ola's Mom? Why? -> She's angry/not happy. Because Ola doesn't want to help her.
C
  • 1. Do you help parents to clean any rooms? What rooms? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. Is it good to clean your room? Why? -> Accept students' own answers

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
cleandirtyfloorshowertoilet
B
bath

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Do you want to…? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't. 2. What do you want to do? (optional, depending on the class) — I want to + verb.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • Do you want to [clean the bath]?
  • Yes. / No.
  • Review: — What do you do in the [room]? — I [action] in the [room].
B
  • I want to [clean the bath/floor/etc.].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Clean or Dirty? Picture Talk — show the clean/messy room pictures one by one (clean kitchen, clean living room, messy bathroom, messy bedroom, messy classroom); students label each "clean" or "dirty" and use "Do you want to [clean the bath]?" — "Yes. / No." exchanges about the pictures. (see below)
  2. Cleaning Charades — one student mimes cleaning the floor, the toilet, the shower or the bath; the others ask "Do you want to [clean the floor]?" and the student answers.
  3. Tidy-Up Race — scatter furniture and rubbish picture cards on the floor; call "Clean the [floor]!"; two teams race to collect the matching cards and say a full sentence for each one.

My Body

Unit 4 · All About Me

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] Point and Name with the Co-teacher: the teacher points to body parts and says "This is my arm."; the co-teacher copies and makes a funny mistake ("Those are my arm."); students correct the co-teacher — accept "this!", pointing and any attempts.
  • [Topical Discussion] Ask students which body parts they use to run, jump or draw; accept gestures and one-word answers in any language, then connect them to the target words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. [Point to Ola's shoulder]. You are Ola. What's this? -> This is my shoulder.
  • 2. [Point to Lele's legs]. You are Lele. What are these? -> These are my legs.
B
  • 1. Where are the kids? -> They are in school.
  • 2. What lesson is it? -> It's PE class.
C
  • 1. [Point at co-teacher's shoulders]. What are these? -> These are his shoulders.
  • 2. What do you do at PE class in your school? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
legarm
B
footfeettoeshoulderpoint

📐 Grammar Focus

1. point to + whose + body part. 2. This is my + singular body part; These are my + plural body parts. 3. This (singular) — These (plural).

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • This is my [body part].
  • These are my [body parts].
B
  • What's this?
  • What's that?
C
  • Point to your [body part].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Freeze Dance Body Parts — play music; students dance freely; when the music stops, call a body part ("Shoulders!"); students freeze, touch it and say "These are my shoulders!"; the last one to freeze and say the sentence sits out one round.
  2. Point Relay Race — two teams line up; call "Point to your toes!"; the first child in each line points, says "These are my toes!" and runs to the back; the fastest team with correct sentences wins.
  3. Simon Says, Full Sentences — play Simon Says with body parts; students only stay in the game if they say the full sentence ("This is my nose.") while touching the part.
  4. Body Trace — in pairs, students trace a hand or a foot on paper and present it: a hand trace goes with "These are my fingers." and a foot trace goes with "These are my toes."

Your Body

Unit 4 · All About Me

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge]* Co-teacher Descriptions: the teacher points to their own arm — "This is my arm." — then points to the co-teacher's arm and stalls on purpose: "This is my…? Hmm — this? No, that! That's my…? No — your! That's your arm!", taking hints from the students; students then point to a partner's body part and try the sentence — accept right vocabulary in wrong sentences.
  • [Topical Discussion] Name That Part: ask students to name the body parts they can see on the teacher or on a partner — accept pointing, Chinese and one-word English answers; connect the "your" idea to pointing at someone else.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. [Point to the photo with Pili's thumb]. You talk to Pili. What's that? -> That's your thumb.
  • 2. [Point to the photo with Laki's knees]. You talk to Laki. What're those? -> Those are your knees.
B
  • 1. What do kids do? -> They look at the pictures.
  • 2.Why do they laugh in the end? -> Because Pili's funny/silly.
C
  • 1. Do you like to take pictures? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. What's your favorite body part? Why? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
toothteeth
B
elbowfingerthumbkneeankle

📐 Grammar Focus

1. That is your + singular body part; Those are your + plural body parts. 2. That (singular) — Those (plural). 3. Distinguish this & that, these & those.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • That's your [body part].
  • Those are your [body parts].
  • Review: This is my [body part]. / These are my [body parts].
B
  • What's that?
  • What are those?

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Point to a Partner's ... — students walk around the room; call "Point to your partner's elbow!" (point from a small distance, no touching); they find a partner, point to the part and say "That's your elbow!"; switch partners each round — cannot be the same two rounds in a row!
  2. Mystery Body Part — one student hides behind a large card or a teacher and sticks out one body part; the class guesses "That's your knee!"; the hidden student confirms or denies.
  3. How Many? — in groups, students count body parts ("How many knees? Eight knees!"); each student then points and says "These are my knees."

Feelings

Unit 4 · All About Me

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] What's Wrong With the Teacher?: the teacher arrives acting out a feeling (rubbing their eyes, rubbing their tummy, stomping); the co-teacher guesses wrong on purpose — "You want a book?"; students help by naming the feeling — accept faces, gestures, Chinese or one-word English answers, and give the English word each time; the situation sets up "How do you feel?".
  • [Topical Discussion] Ask students how they feel today; accept faces, gestures and one-word answers in any language, then connect them to the feeling words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. [Point to Lele]. You are Lele. How do you feel? -> I feel sleepy.
  • 2. [Point to Ola]. How does he feel? -> He feels sleepy.
B
  • 1. Where are the kids? -> They are in school.
  • 2. Is Laki really angry? -> No, he isn't. It's a play.
C
  • 1. How do you feel? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. What feelings can you show? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
angryexcitedhungryfeel
B
sleepythirsty

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Ask and answer How do you feel? — I feel + feeling. Supplement (classes that covered SE): How does he/she feel? — He/She feels…

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • How do you feel?
  • I feel [feeling].
B
  • He/She feels [feeling].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Feelings Charades — one student secretly picks a feeling card and acts it out; the class asks "How do you feel?"; the actor answers "I feel [hungry]!"; rotate actors quickly to keep the energy up.
  2. Feelings Chain — students sit in a circle; the first asks the next "How do you feel?"; the answerer says "I feel thirsty!" and asks the next; no repeats allowed until all six target words are used; if someone repeats, the group counts the used feelings on their fingers as a cue and the student tries again — everyone stays in the chain.
  3. Die of Feelings — a student throws a die, makes the matching face and says "I feel [excited]!"; the class repeats; add a silly rule (on "sleepy", everyone pretends to snore for three seconds).
  4. Feeling Corners — place the six feeling cards in different corners; describe a situation ("It's bedtime." / "You dropped your ice cream."); students move to a corner and say "I feel [sleepy]."

My Toys

Unit 4 · All About Me

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] My Favourite Toy: the teacher mimes playing with a favourite toy and hugs it: "I like my…?"; take students' hints to land on "I like my [toy]."; then mime dropping a toy: "I don't like…?"; accept single toy words in English or Chinese.
  • [Topical Discussion] Ask students what toys they like at home; accept one-word answers and Chinese, then translate them into English.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. You are Ola. Do you like your soft toy? -> No, I don't.
  • 2. Does Lele like marbles? -> Yes, she does.
B
  • 1. Why doesn't Ola like his soft toy? -> It's old/broken.
  • 2.What toy does Nani like? -> Nani likes her puzzle.
C
  • 1. What toys do you like? What toys don't you like? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. Do you share your toys with your friends? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

B
blockscardsmarblespuzzlesoft toy
C
board game

📐 Grammar Focus

1. I like + your/my + noun. 2. I don't like + your/my + noun.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • I like [your marbles].
  • I don't like [my soft toy].
B
  • Do you like [my puzzle]?

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Toy Shop Role-Play — display the six toy flashcards (or real toys) as a shop; student A is the shopkeeper ("I like my blocks!"); student B responds "I like your blocks!" or "I don't like your blocks!"; then swap roles.
  2. Like / Don't Like Line-Up — call a toy; students run to the LIKE side or the DON'T LIKE side of the room and say the full sentence; check both sides each round.
  3. Mystery Bag — put real toys or flashcards in a bag; a student pulls one out and says "I like my [...]!" or "I don't like my [...]!".
  4. Draw Your Favourite Toy — students draw their favourite toy (real or imaginary) and tell a partner "I like my [...]."; partners ask "Do you like my [...]?"

Bugs

Unit 5 · Animals

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] There's a Bug in the Classroom!: the co-teacher looks through the lesson flashcards and suddenly panics — "What's that?! A bug! Eek!"; the teacher investigates and wonders aloud "A bug? What is it?"; co-teacher mimes to explain the word; students help identify the (pretend) insect — accept Chinese names and buzzing sounds, and give the English word each time; the panic creates the need for the bug words.
  • [Topical Discussion] Ask students which bugs they know or have seen; accept Chinese names and translate them into English.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. [Point to a ladybug]. What's this? -> It's a ladybug.
  • 2. What bug does Lele like? -> She likes a beetle.
B
  • 1. How does Ola feel? Why? -> He feels angry. He doesn't like this fly.
  • 2. Are other kids happy? Why? -> They like to look at bugs.
C
  • 1. Do you like bugs? Which bug do you like/don't you like? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. What do you do when you go to a park? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
beefly
B
antbeetleladybugbug

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Review a/an before nouns. 2. A/An + animal + is a bug. 3. How to classify (fruit, bug, animal, toy, fish, bird) — with the courseware's Look and Say.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • A [bee] is a [bug].
  • An [ant] is a [bug].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Bugs Videos — watch the two insect clips from the DingTalk cloud folder; pause on each insect and ask "What is it?"; students answer "A [bee] is a bug." (see below)
  2. Bug Hunt — hide the bug flashcards around the room; students find them and say "A [ladybug] is a bug."; the fastest collector with correct sentences wins.
  3. Bug or Not? — show a mix of creatures and objects; students decide together whether each one is a bug and answer with "An [ant] is a bug." or "A [dog] is not a bug."
  4. Move Like a Bug — call an insect; students move like it (crawl like an ant, buzz like a bee); when the music stops they freeze and say "I'm a [bee]. A bee is a bug."

Birds

Unit 5 · Animals

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] Bird or Bug?: the co-teacher mimes an insect from the last lesson and the class reviews — "A [bee] is a bug!"; then the co-teacher suddenly flaps like a bird — "A [bee] is a bug? And a bird? Is a duck a bug?"; the wrong classification creates the need for "Is a [...] a bird?".
  • [Topical Discussion]* Pets and Animals: ask students about pets or animals at home; test each one with the class: "Is a [cat] a bird?" — "No, it isn't!"; only a yes/no answer is expected.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. Is an eagle a bird? -> Yes, it is.
  • 2. Are ducks birds? -> Yes, they are.
B
  • 1. Why does the teacher say "Let's be quiet!"? -> Because the owl is sleeping.
  • 2. Who wakes up an owl? -> A parrot.
C
  • 1. Review the U4C14 near/far contrast (this/these vs that/those) with the correct be-verb, with minimal support.
  • 2. A penguin can't fly. Is a penguin a bird? -> Yes, it is — penguins are birds that can't fly. The question has a factual answer; guide students to it rather than accepting opinions.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
duck
B
eaglegooseowlparrot
C
geese

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Is a/an + animal + a bug/bird? — Yes, it is. / No, it isn't. 2. Review be-verb use after This-These / That-Those.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • Is a [duck] a [bird]?
  • Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
  • Review: A [bee] is a [bug].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Bird or Bug or Pet? (HTML) — an animal appears on screen; students name it and classify it as a bird, a bug or a pet, practising "Is a [parrot] a bird?" and the answers "Yes, it is. / No, it isn't." (see below)
  2. Bird or Bug Sort — teams sort animal picture cards into birds and bugs on the board, saying "A [duck] is a bird." or "A [bee] is a bug." for each card.
  3. Animal Twenty Questions — one student secretly chooses an animal; the class asks up to twenty "Is it a [bird]?" questions; the student answers "Yes, it is. / No, it isn't." until the animal is guessed.
  4. Flap or Crawl — ask "Is a [goose] a bird?"; if yes, students flap their wings; if no, they squat; keep everyone in the game — give a cue and have the group repeat the correct move together instead of sitting anyone out.

Large Animals

Unit 5 · Animals

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] Is a Bear a Bird?: review the last lesson — "Is a duck a bird? Yes, it is!" — then the teacher grows huge arms like a bear — "Is a bear a bird? Hmm... a bear is a...?"; take hints in any language from the students and land on "A bear is a large animal.".
  • [Topical Discussion] Ask students about the biggest animal they know; accept Chinese names and translate them into English.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. Is a bear a large animal? -> Yes, a bear is a large animal.
  • 2. What is a giraffe? -> A giraffe is a large animal.
B
  • 1. What does hippo like to do? -> It likes to swim.
  • 2. How does Ola feel? Why? -> Sad/cold/angry, because baby elephant washes him.
C
  • 1. Do you like to go to the zoo? What animals can you see in the zoo? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. Do you like large animals? What's your favourite? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
bearelephantgiraffeanimallarge
B
hippo

📐 Grammar Focus

1. What is a/an + animal? — A/An + animal is a large/small animal. 2. Review classification.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • What is a [bear]?
  • A [bear] is a large animal.
  • Review: Is a [duck] a [bird]? — Yes, it is.

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Large Animal Videos — watch the animal-size clips from the courseware folder; pause on each large animal and ask "What is it?"; students answer "A [giraffe] is a large animal." (see below)
  2. Size Sort — teams sort the unit's animal cards into large and small piles; each card is announced with "A [...] is a large/small animal." (completed in C20).
  3. Animal Riddles — describe a large animal one feature at a time ("It is large. It has a long neck."); students guess and answer with the full sentence "A [giraffe] is a large animal."
  4. Draw and Guess — students draw a large animal in secret; the class asks "Is it a [hippo]?"; the artist answers, then reveals the drawing and says "A [...] is a large animal."

Small Animals

Unit 5 · Animals

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] Large or Small?: review the last lesson with big gestures — "A bear is a large animal!" — then shrink the gestures for a frog: "A frog is a large animal? No! A frog is a…?"; let students solve the problem and land on "small animal".
  • [Topical Discussion] Ask students about small animals they know; accept any answers and connect them to the lesson words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. [Point to a hamster]. Is it a rat? -> No, this is a hamster.
  • 2. Is a worm a small animal? -> Yes, a worm is a small animal.
B

C
  • 1. Do you have any pet animals? What are they? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. What's your favourite small animal? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
small
B
crabfroghamsterratworm

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Is a/an + animal + a large/small animal? — Yes, a/an… is a large/small animal. / No, a/an… is a large/small animal.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • Is a [crab] a small animal?
  • Yes, a [crab] is a small animal.
  • Review: A [bear] is a large animal.

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  4. Unit Animal Bingo — students fill bingo cards with unit animals; the teacher gives a description ("It is a small animal. It jumps."); students cover the animal and say its sentence.
