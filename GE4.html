GE4 U1C1

What Does He Do?

Unit 1 · People and Places

💡 Main Idea — what students should be able to do

Demonstrate a job-identification exchange: each student independently asks “What does s/he do?” and answers “S/he’s a [job].”, using the correct pronoun and target job word.

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] Wrong Job, Right Job: teacher mimes a scene which requires different jobs (going to the shop, getting something stolen, having a fire). Co-teacher is coming to help. Then, teacher asks "What do you do?", and co-teacher names a wrong job. This repeats a few times. Then, the teacher asks the students, trying to elicit the correct job in any language, then says "S/he is a (correct job)".
  • [Topical Discussion] What Do Your Parents Do?: ask students what jobs their parents or family members do ("I'm a teacher. What about your mom?"); accept one-word answers in any language, then translate them into English ("Ola's Mom is a shopkeeper!")

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. What does Mr. Bunn do? -> He's a baker.
  • 2. [Point to the cake]. What is it? -> It's a cake.
B
  • 1. Is Ola really a policeman? -> No, he is not. He's a student.
  • 2. What does Ola's dad do? -> He's a policeman.
C
  • 1. What does your mom/your dad do? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. Does Miss Mills like Mr Burns? Why or why not? -> Accept students' own answers. (Yes, because her face is pink./No, because she wants to eat the cake, not talk to him.)

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
farmerfiremanpoliceman
B
bakerpostmanshopkeeper
C
MrMissMrs

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Ask about jobs with the pattern “What does s/he do?” — “S/he is + a/an….” (no an in this lesson). 2. Review the meaning of she/he (new-class teaching). 3. Read and understand Mr./Miss./Mrs. in the text.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • — What does he do?
  • — He’s a [job].
  • — What does she do?
  • — She’s a [job].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Who’s Who? (Job Hats) — In groups of three, students wear job hats. Two ask and answer about the third: “What does s/he do?” — “S/he’s a baker.” Rotate roles until everyone has asked, answered and been described. Optional: mime the job. Remind students to include a.
  2. Jobs Quiz (HTML) — Students identify jobs from pictures and match sentences to pictures. After each answer, reveal the dialogue and practise “What does s/he do?” — “S/he’s a [job].” (see below)

🏠 Homework Ideas

  1. Using the teacher's example, record a dialogue with your parents or friends using a target structure.
  2. Read the words, the dialogue and the story from the book. Extension 1: make your own sentences with the target vocabulary and record them for the teacher. Extension 2: read the story with expression and/or role play the story with your friends or parents.
  3. Extension: ask a family member about another family member “What does s/he do?” and report back in English: “S/he’s a [job].”

GE4 U1C2

Where Does He Work?

Unit 1 · People and Places

💡 Main Idea — what students should be able to do

Demonstrate a connected job-and-workplace exchange: each student independently asks and answers “What does s/he do?” and “Where does s/he work?”, using the correct pronoun, works and a/an.

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] Wrong Address: review the jobs with quick mimes or flashcards — “He’s a baker!” — then ask where the baker works and guess wrong on purpose (you can use flashcards to help): “A farm? A school?”; take hints in any language from the students and land on “He works in a bakery.” — the workplace words solve the problem.
  • [Topical Discussion] Where Do They Go?: ask students where their parents or family members go to work; accept one-word answers in any language, then connect them to the workplace words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. Where does Mr. Bell work? -> He works in a post office.
  • 2. Where does your teacher work? -> S/he works in a school.
B
  • 1. What does Mr Burns do? -> He's a fireman.
  • 2. What does Lele want to eat? -> She wants to eat some bread.
C
  • 1. Where does your mother/father work? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. Who works in a fire station/police station/etc.? -> A fireman works in a fire station.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
officework
B
bakeryfire stationpolice stationpost office
C
doctor (HTML)office worker (HTML)pilot (HTML)

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Where asks about a place. 2. Ask about workplaces: “Where does s/he work?” — “S/he works in + a/an….”; stress the -s on works in speech. 3. Review a/an (new-class teaching).

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • — Where does he work?
  • — He works in a/an [workplace].
  • — Where does she work?
  • — She works in a/an [workplace].
  • Review:
  • — What does s/he do?
  • — S/he’s a [job].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Get to Work! — A “lost worker” wears a job hat while two classmates ask about the job and workplace, then lead the worker to the matching place card in the corner of the classroom. Students rotate roles and can swap hats for new jobs.
  2. Job and Workplace Match — students match job cards to suitable workplace pictures, then ask and answer both “What does s/he do?” and “Where does s/he work?”.
  3. Find Your Partner — give half the students person/job cards and half workplace cards; students find a suitable match, then another pair asks them about the person’s job and workplace.
  4. Busy City! (HTML) — Students match workers to workplaces and ask and answer “What does s/he do?” and “Where does s/he work?” using full sentences. Rotate speaking partners and cover all target words. Reveal labels and sentence prompts only as needed. (see below)

🏠 Homework Ideas

  1. Using the teacher's example, record a dialogue with your parents or friends using a target structure.
  2. Read the words, the dialogue and the story from the book. Extension 1: make your own sentences with the target vocabulary and record them for the teacher. Extension 2: read the story with expression and/or role play the story with your friends or parents.
  3. Extension: draw a family member at their workplace and say two sentences: “She’s a [job]. She works in a [workplace].”

Ola’s Busy Day

Unit 1 · People and Places

💡 Main Idea — what students should be able to do

Demonstrate a destination exchange: each student independently asks “Where do you go?” and answers with a familiar destination, using “I go to the library.” or “I go to school.” appropriately.

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] Rescue the Bored Teacher: the co-teacher mimes being bored (yawning, stretching, saying "Soooo boring"); the teacher asks and mimes “Where do you want to go?” and the co-teacher shrugs; try to have students suggest places (park, zoo, school) — accept any language, and the teacher repeats each idea in English with “I go to the…”; the boredom creates the need for the place words.
  • [Topical Discussion] Your Weekend: ask students where they go on weekends; accept gestures and one-word answers in any language, then connect them to the place words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. Is Ola busy today? -> Yes, he is.
  • 2. Where does Ola go? -> He goes to school. He goes to the library. He goes to the park. He goes home.
B
  • 1. Why is Ola sleepy? -> Because he is so busy.
  • 2. Why does Ola go to the park? -> He wants to climb a tree.
C
  • 1. Are you busy today? Where do you go? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. Why do you go to the park/the library/bakery/shop? -> Accept students' own answers.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
busydaygo tolibrarystory
B
climb

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Review where (new-class teaching). 2. Where asks about destinations: “Where do you go?” — “I go to + place.” 3. Understand the meaning of “go to school”.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • — I go to the [place].
  • I go to school.
B
  • — Where do you go?

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Where Do You Go? — place familiar destination cards around the room; students choose a card and ask and answer with a partner.
  2. Destination Reveal — slowly reveal a place picture; students identify it and say “I go to the…” or “I go to school.”, then ask a partner the question.
  3. Where Do You Go? (HTML) — Teacher (or a student) moves a tiny window to guess hidden places and say “I go to the…” using full sentences before revealing the picture. Add an extra challenge by asking “Who works in the…?” and sending the worker in after students answer. Explore all 11 places and reveal sentence prompts only as needed. (see below)

🏠 Homework Ideas

  1. Using the teacher's example, record a dialogue with your parents or friends using a target structure.
  2. Read the words, the dialogue and the story from the book. Extension 1: make your own sentences with the target vocabulary and record them for the teacher. Extension 2: read the story with expression and/or role play the story with your friends or parents.
  3. Extension: tell a family member three places you go during the week using “I go to the [place].” / “I go to school.”
  4. Extension: visit a few places from the lesson in real life. Record a video with a student in front of the place saying the sentence "I go to (the) [place]".

Laki’s Dad

Unit 1 · People and Places

💡 Main Idea — what students should be able to do

Demonstrate a person-and-destination exchange: each student independently asks “Where does s/he go?” and answers “S/he goes to [country].”, using the correct pronoun, verb form and target country name.

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] Same Trip, New Sentence: review “Where do you go?” — “I go to (the) [place].” with gestures and flashcards; then point at the co-teacher and stall on purpose: “I go? No — he! He… go? Hmm…”; take hints in any language from the students and complete the sentence yourself: “S/he goes to…” — the third-person form is the new problem.
  • [Topical Discussion] Flags and Places: ask which countries or faraway places students know; accept Chinese names and translate them into English, then connect them to the country words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1. What does Laki's dad do? -> He's a pilot.
  • 2. [Point to the place]. Where does he go? -> He goes to China.
B
  • 1. Why does he like England? -> Because it's an old country.
  • 2. Why can he go to all of these places? -> Because he's a pilot.
C
  • 1. What country do you want to go? -> Accept students' own answers.
  • 2. What country do you like? Why? -> Accept students' own answers; try not to limit them to China, help to translate from Chinese when needed.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
Chinafly (v)
B
AmericaEnglandJapancountrypilot

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Review “Where do you go? — I go to + place.” 2. Practise “Where does s/he go? — S/he goes….”; stress the form in speech.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • — He goes to [country].
  • — She goes to [country].
  • Review and use in a new context:
  • — Where do you go?
  • — I go to [country].
B
  • — Where does s/he go?

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Pilot, Pilot, Where Do You Go? — Place country flags in the four corners of the room. One student (the “pilot”) stands in the middle with eyes closed. Everyone else quietly walks to different corners, then the class chants: “Pilot, pilot, where do you go?” The pilot calls out “I go to [country]!” and everyone in that corner should sit down. The remaining students point at the pilot and say “S/he goes to [country]!”
  2. Mystery Destination — Call one student to the whiteboard and ask them to turn away from the class. Show a country flashcard to the rest of the class and ask: “Where does s/he go?” The class (or a chosen student) answers with the full sentence. Then ask the turned-away student: “Where do you go?” — they must answer “I go to [country]!”
  3. Go or Goes? (HTML interactive) — Show a destination and switch the subject card between I, He, and She; students say the sentence with go or goes. Three levels: countries only → add workplaces → add workers as subjects. (see below)
  4. Verb Swap — say an I sentence and ask students to change the subject to s/he/worker; students should say the new sentence, using an -s/-es guide if needed.

🏠 Homework Ideas

  1. Using the teacher's example, record a dialogue with your parents or friends using a target structure.
  2. Read the words, the dialogue and the story from the book. Extension 1: make your own sentences with the target vocabulary and record them for the teacher. Extension 2: read the story with expression and/or role play the story with your friends or parents.
  3. Extension: find pictures or flags of the target countries in a book or at home; say one sentence for each: “He goes to [Japan].”

A Video Game

Unit 2 · Getting Around

💡 Main Idea — what students should be able to do

Demonstrate following directions: each student independently gives a classmate two-step directions — “Go left, then go right!” — and follows a partner’s directions.

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] Blindfold Walk: the co-teacher closes their eyes and must reach a chair; the teacher first tries “Go! Go!” — it doesn’t work; students help with “left!” “right!” — accept pointing and Chinese, and give the English word each time; the failed guiding creates the need for the direction words.
  • [Interactive Game] Simon Says Directions: play Simon Says with direction gestures — hands up, hands down, point left, point right; students only move on “Simon says”; accept gestures and giggles, then connect them to the direction words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

—

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
updownleftrightcoolgame
B
pick up

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Go + direction word gives directions: go left, go right, go up; supplement: go straight.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • — Go left!
  • — Go right!
B
  • — Go left, then go right!
  • — Where do I go?*

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Classroom Obstacle Course: place chairs or flashcards as obstacles; one student closes their eyes and a partner guides them to a target with “Go left! Go right! Go up! Go down!”; a wrong turn just gets a new instruction. Swap roles and targets.
  2. Direction Draw: students draw a simple path on a mini-whiteboard while the teacher calls out instructions; partners compare maps, then one student gives instructions and the other draws.
  3. Program the Teacher: students give the teacher directions to reach an object in the classroom; the teacher follows the instructions exactly — with funny results when they are wrong; repeat in pairs with a toy.

🏠 Homework Ideas

  1. Using the teacher's example, record a dialogue with your parents or friends using a target structure.
  2. Read the words, the dialogue and the story from the book. Extension 1: make your own sentences with the target vocabulary and record them for the teacher. Extension 2: read the story with expression and/or role play the story with your friends or parents.
  3. Extension: guide a family member to find something at home using “Go left!”, “Go right!” and “then”.

Lele’s City

Unit 2 · Getting Around

💡 Main Idea — what students should be able to do

Demonstrate an observation exchange: each student independently asks “What can you see?” and answers “I can see a/an [place].”, naming the target city places.

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Topical Discussion] Our Neighbourhood: ask students what places are near their home — shops, hospitals, banks; accept one-word answers in any language, then connect them to the city words.
  • [Interactive Game] Picture Search: show the city picture for a few seconds; ask “What can you see?”; students call out whatever they spotted — accept one-word answers in any language before modelling “I can see a…”.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

—

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
bankcitygreatlightstore
B
hospital

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Review a/an: a/an goes with a singular noun. 2. Oral practice: “What can you see?” — “I can see a/an….”.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • — What can you see?
  • — I can see a/an [place].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. City Picture Walk: show a big city picture (or the courseware scene); pairs take turns asking “What can you see?” and answering “I can see a/an…”; switch partners after two rounds to cover all places. (see below)
  2. Build a City: small groups arrange place flashcards to build a paper city on the desk; groups visit each other’s cities and ask and answer about what they can see.
  3. Sound and Guess: play or make sounds connected to places (a car horn, an ambulance); ask “What can you hear?” and “What can you see?”; students answer with full sentences.

🏠 Homework Ideas

  1. Using the teacher's example, record a dialogue with your parents or friends using a target structure.
  2. Read the words, the dialogue and the story from the book. Extension 1: make your own sentences with the target vocabulary and record them for the teacher. Extension 2: read the story with expression and/or role play the story with your friends or parents.
  3. Extension: draw a small map of your neighbourhood with three places; point and say “I can see a [hospital].”

What Can You Hear?

Unit 2 · Getting Around

💡 Main Idea — what students should be able to do

Demonstrate a sound-guessing exchange: each student independently asks “Is it a [vehicle]?” and answers “Yes, it’s a [vehicle]. / No, it’s a [vehicle].”, using the target vehicle words.

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Problem Situation] Behind the Door: the teacher makes vehicle sounds from behind a book or the classroom door — “Vroom! Vroom!” — and asks “What is it?”; students guess — accept Chinese and sound effects, and give the English word each time; the guessing creates the need for the vehicle words.
  • [Topical Discussion] How Do You Come to School?: ask how students come to school — by bus, by car, on foot; accept gestures and one-word answers in any language, then connect them to the vehicle words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

—

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
boatbuscarplanetrainloudnear

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Review a/an. 2. Oral practice: “Is it a/an…?” — “Yes, it is.” (full form) / “No, it isn’t.” (contracted form).

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • — Is it a [vehicle]?
  • — Yes, it’s a [vehicle].
  • — No, it’s a [vehicle].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Vehicle Sound Quiz: make or play vehicle sounds; students ask “Is it a [plane]?” and answer “Yes, it’s a plane. / No, it’s a…”; every student gets a turn to make a sound.
  2. Vehicle Charades: a student mimes a vehicle; the class asks “Is it a [bus]?” until they guess, then answers with the full sentence; rotate quickly.
  3. Class Traffic: students walk around the room as their favourite vehicles; the teacher calls “Stop! Is it a car?” and nearby students answer; keep everyone moving and speaking.
  4. What Can You Hear? (HTML) — plays real recorded sounds from the lesson folder (14 sounds: vehicles, animals, others; on-screen toggles choose the mix); students listen, ask “Is it a [vehicle]?” and answer, then choose the correct word out of four; after the fanfare the sound repeats, a 3-2-1 countdown leads to the dialogue “— What can you hear? — Is it a [bus]? — Yes, it is.” (first line optional via toggle; optional “long dialogue” for stronger groups adds a wrong guess: “Is it a bus? — No, it’s not!” before the correct question; ⏹ stop button for the sound). 🏠 Homework Ideas

  1. Using the teacher's example, record a dialogue with your parents or friends using a target structure.
  2. Read the words, the dialogue and the story from the book. Extension 1: make your own sentences with the target vocabulary and record them for the teacher. Extension 2: read the story with expression and/or role play the story with your friends or parents.
  3. Extension: listen for vehicle sounds on the way home and tell a family member what you heard with “It’s a [bus].”

Answering Questions

Unit 2 · Getting Around

💡 Main Idea — what students should be able to do

Demonstrate a description exchange: each student independently asks “Is it [adjective]?” and answers “Yes, it is. / No, it isn’t.”, using the target adjectives.

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] Same or Different?: review the vehicle guessing game — “Is it a bus? Yes, it’s a bus!” — then point at a car and a bus and ask “The same? No! A car is…? A bus is…?”; take hints in any language from the students and land on “fast” and “slow” — the new adjectives describe, not name.
  • [Topical Discussion] Loud or Quiet?: ask about sounds students like — “Music — loud or quiet?”; accept gestures such as covering ears and single words in any language, then connect them to the adjective words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

—

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
answercutefastnewquietslow

📐 Grammar Focus

1. Opposite-adjective exchange: “Is it fast?” — “Yes, it’s fast.” / “No, it’s slow.” 2. Oral practice with opposite pairs: fast & slow, new & old, loud & quiet, near & far, big & small, long & short, black & white.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • — Is it [adjective]?
  • — Yes, it’s [adjective].
  • — No, it’s [adjective].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Opposite Actions: the teacher calls an adjective (fast, slow, quiet, loud) and students move around the room that way; then pairs ask and answer “Is it [fast]?” about classroom objects or pictures.
  2. Adjective Picture Sort: show object or vehicle pictures; pairs sort them into fast/slow, loud/quiet, new/old columns, saying “It’s fast. / It isn’t fast.” for each.
  3. Guess the Object: one student thinks of a classroom object; the class asks “Is it quiet?” “Is it new?” to guess it; the student answers with “Yes, it is. / No, it isn’t.”

🏠 Homework Ideas

  1. Using the teacher's example, record a dialogue with your parents or friends using a target structure.
  2. Read the words, the dialogue and the story from the book. Extension 1: make your own sentences with the target vocabulary and record them for the teacher. Extension 2: read the story with expression and/or role play the story with your friends or parents.
  3. Extension: describe two things at home with the lesson adjectives: “It’s quiet. It isn’t loud.”

Fruit

Unit 4 · Food

💡 Main Idea — what students should be able to do

Demonstrate a fruit-preference exchange: each student independently asks “What fruit do you like?” and answers “I like [fruit]. / I don’t like [fruit].”, using the target fruit words.

🎯 Lead-in Ideas

  • [Title Slide Talk] Show the title slide and ask some A-level questions.
  • [Review Bridge] Same Question, New Words: review “What do you like eating?” with the food words; then hold up an apple — “Apples! Apple is a fruit. What fruit do you like?”; the familiar question with a new word group is today’s target.
  • [Topical Discussion] Yummy or Yucky?: name a fruit and ask “Yummy? Yucky?”; students make a yummy or yucky face — accept faces and gestures, then connect them to the fruit words.

📖 Story Questions (samples)

A
  • 1.You are Nani. What fruit do you like? What fruit don't you like? -> I like mangoes, pineapples, pears and plums. I don't like lemons.
  • 2. Where is the watermelon? -> It's under the table.
B
  • 1. Why does Nani's father laugh? -> Becaues Nani wears a cook's/baker's hat.
  • 2. What do they do? -> They cut fruits.
C
  • 1. What fruit do you like? -> Accept student's own answers.
  • 2. Is Nani a good girl? Why? -> Yes, because she helps her parents.

🎯 Target Vocabulary

A
pearwatermelonlemon
B
mango
C
pineappleplum

📐 Grammar Focus

1. “What fruit do you like?” — “I like + plural.” 2. Review the plural rules: usually add -s; add -es after s, x, sh, ch; the y-rules. 3. New: nouns ending in o — living things add -es, non-living things add -s.

🗣 Target Structures

A
  • — What fruit do you like?
  • — I like [fruit].
B
  • I don’t like [fruit].

🎲 Bonus Activity Ideas

  1. Fruit Survey: walk and ask classmates “What fruit do you like?”; note answers and report: “Anna likes [mangoes].”
  2. Taste Mime: teacher pretends to give students different fruits; students mime tasting fruits — yum or yuck faces — and say “I like [lemons]. / I don’t like [lemons].”
  3. Fruit Market: pairs run a fruit stall with flashcards or fruit toys; customers ask the question and answer with likes or dislikes, then swap roles.
  4. Fruit Mystery (HTML) — the screen shows a big fruit with its name hidden; students say the word, then make and say “I like [fruit]s. / I don’t like [fruit]s.” before tapping to reveal, then ask a partner “What fruit do you like?”. File: GE4 U4C14. Fruit Mystery.html. (see below)

🏠 Homework Ideas

  1. Using the teacher's example, record a dialogue with your parents or friends using a target structure.
  2. Read the words, the dialogue and the story from the book. Extension 1: make your own sentences with the target vocabulary and record them for the teacher. Extension 2: read the story with expression and/or role play the story with your friends or parents.
  3. Extension: ask two family members “What fruit do you like?” and report their answers.
